# Matisse Art

Produces original Matisse-inspired fine art for home decor — wall art, framed prints, canvas art, gallery-wall pieces, and exhibition posters — in three visual languages. The goal is decor-ready artwork that feels like a warm, collectible modern-art piece: joyful color, simplified form, hand-made texture.

Default output is ARTWORK-ONLY: full-bleed art, no poster text, no white border, no signature/caption. Switch to **exhibition-poster** layout when the user asks for a poster, museum print, gallery exhibition look, or typed title/venue lines. The art itself stays original; type and mats are a presentation layer, not a copy of a famous poster.

## The three Matisse languages — choose first

Decide which language fits the request before composing. They are visually distinct; don't blend them into one muddy image. Exception: language C contour lines may sit on language A color-fields only when the user asks for a mixed abstract portrait (see Hybrid).

**A. Cut-outs / Papiers Découpés (late Matisse) — the default for decor.** Flat, solid shapes that look cut from painted gouache paper and arranged by hand. Organic abstract forms: leaves, coral, flowers, doves/birds, reclining or dancing figures, suns, waves. Bold saturated silhouette, generous negative space, subtle paper grain/rough torn edges, no perspective, no shading. Ground is usually warm off-white/cream, sometimes a single flat color panel. This is the calm, graphic, very decor-friendly mode. Choose it for abstract botanicals, animals, figures, minimal statements, gallery sets, and most exhibition posters.

**B. Fauvist / painterly (early Matisse).** Loose, visible brushwork; bright, non-naturalistic color used for emotion rather than realism; flattened perspective. Subjects: open windows looking onto color-blocked landscapes, cheerful interiors (pink floors, pastel walls, simple chairs/vases), figures and nudes in gardens, Mediterranean scenes. Hand-painted edges, sketchy under-drawing allowed, color fields of green/coral/purple/blue/pink. Choose it when the user wants a painted scene, interior, landscape, or a warmer expressive feel.

**C. Contour / brush-ink drawing.** Extreme economy of line: thick calligraphic black brush-ink strokes on warm cream paper, visible pressure and bristle texture, almost no interior modeling. Portraits and figures reduced to a few essential contours — jaw, hair mass, eyes as dark marks, nose as one angular stroke. Strictly monochrome (black on cream) unless the user asks for color. Choose it for line portraits, "ink drawing," "Matisse drawing," or Nadia-like faces. Do not turn this into a realistic sketch or a thin digital outline.

If the user isn't sure, default to cut-outs for single graphic prints, painterly for scene-based art, contour for portraits/line drawings; offer another language as an alternative rather than asking unnecessarily.

**Hybrid (only if asked):** cut-out color fields plus a contour face — a grainy yellow (or cream) face-shape, cobalt contour features, a few satellite organic shapes. Do not use this as the default.

## Output mode — artwork-only vs exhibition poster

1. **Artwork-only (default).** Full-bleed image. No typography, signatures, titles, edition numbers, or white/cream mat.
2. **Exhibition poster.** Use when the user wants a poster, exhibition print, museum/gallery look, or named title. Follow [Exhibition poster layout](#exhibition-poster-layout).

## Composition & design rules

- Simplify ruthlessly. Matisse reduces a subject to its essential silhouette. A flower is a few rounded petal-shapes; a body is one flowing contour; a bird is two or three angled planes; a coral leaf is a stem with rounded finger-lobes. Favor a few large shapes over many small details.
- Flatness over realism. No realistic lighting, gradients, 3D rendering, phototexture, or perspective. Cut-outs are pure flat color; painterly work uses flat color patches with visible brush marks, not modeled volume; contour work is line only.
- Negative space is a design element. Let the cream/ground breathe around forms; don't fill the frame edge-to-edge with detail.
- Balance by eye, asymmetrically. One large anchoring form plus a smaller counter-form (e.g. a big coral leaf with a small sun circle; two doves in dynamic pairing; a yellow frond with a small teal oval).
- Hand-made imperfection. Slightly irregular, wobbly, organic edges; subtle paper fiber/grain; torn-paper feel. Avoid clean vector-perfect digital shapes and avoid a polished AI-render look.
- Decor harmony. The piece should hang well in a room: strong enough to read from across a space, calm enough to live with. High contrast where it counts, limited overall palette.

### Cut-out composition recipes (original forms)

Use these structures; invent new silhouettes — do not clone a reference leaf.

- **Single frond on a color panel:** one large coral/leaf/seaweed silhouette, centered in a flat inner square or rectangle (periwinkle, celadon, powder blue, grey, tan, cream).
- **Frond + sun:** large sage/green leaf with a mustard-yellow circle tucked behind upper lobes.
- **Pinwheel:** three or four related fronds meeting at the center, warm ochre/terracotta/peach.
- **Dance ring:** several simplified dancing figures as flat one-color silhouettes on cream — original poses, not a copy of *The Dance*.
- **Overlap the frame:** the silhouette may slightly break the inner panel or mat edge.

## Color

Matisse color is bold, flat, and joyful — never muddy or neon. Build from a small set (typically 3–5 colors, often fewer on posters) and let one or two dominate.

- **Cut-outs palette:** cobalt / ultramarine blue, warm coral red, hot pink, soft pink, peach/orange, golden yellow, leaf/forest/sage green, periwinkle, celadon/seafoam, terracotta, on cream / off-white / pale beige ground.
- **Poster pairs that read well:** orange on periwinkle; pink on celadon; yellow on cream with peach mat; teal on pale grey; sage + mustard sun on cream; black on sandy beige; red figures on cream.
- **Fauvist palette:** bright greens, coral/terracotta, lavender/purple, sky and deep blue, pink, ochre/cream — applied in non-naturalistic patches (a pink floor, a green face of landscape, a purple wall).
- **Contour palette:** black ink on warm cream paper. Type (if poster) matches the ink.
- Prefer a warm off-white/cream ground over pure white for a softer, more collectible feel.
- Keep saturation high but tasteful; colors sit flat next to each other rather than blending. On posters, type is usually black; on warm terracotta palettes, copper-brown type is allowed.

## Motifs (original, not copied)

Draw on Matisse's recurring subjects but always compose original forms — never reproduce a famous artwork (Blue Nude, *The Dance*, Nadia portraits, specific doves, a known Berggruen/Maeght poster) verbatim.

- **Cut-outs:** abstract leaves & coral fronds, simplified flowers with oversized petals, doves/birds in flight, sun and circle motifs, waves, reclining/dancing abstract figures, acrobats.
- **Painterly:** open shuttered windows with color-blocked landscape beyond, interiors with chair/table/vase, gardens and olive trees, nudes/figures at rest, still lifes, Mediterranean coast.
- **Contour:** simplified faces and busts, reclining nudes in a few strokes, still-life contours. Invent the sitter; do not redraw Nadia or other named portraits.

## Exhibition poster layout

When poster mode is on, compose a vertical museum print — not a busy graphic-design poster.

**Structure (top to bottom on a warm cream sheet):**

1. **Header** — centered high-contrast serif: `MATISSE` in large bold caps with generous tracking. Subtitle on the next line in smaller italic serif: `PAPIERS DÉCOUPÉS`, `THE CUT-OUTS`, or an original title.
2. **Art field** — a centered inner panel (cream, or a single flat color) holding the artwork. Wide cream mat around it. Optional: the main shape slightly overlaps the panel.
3. **Footer** — smaller centered serif: gallery + city, exhibition dates, or a short original line. Warm palettes may use copper-brown type instead of black.

**Type rules**

- Serif only; letterspaced caps for the name. No sans-serif UI type, no script except a faint ink signature in contour posters.
- Invent original titles and plausible venue lines. Do not copy real exhibition copy (`BERGGRUEN & CIE` 1953 dates, `NADIA AUX CHEVEUX LISSES`, `Salon d'Automne` *The Dance* layout, lorem ipsum).
- Artwork-only requests: omit all of this.

## Generation workflow

Confirm the essentials only if they change the deliverable: subject/motif, language (infer if obvious), output mode (artwork-only vs poster), size/aspect, and whether it's a single print or a coordinated set. Don't over-ask; motif and mood can be inferred and refined.

**Single print:** one `GenerateImage` call. Default aspect ratio for wall art is portrait (`3:4`) unless the user specifies square (`1:1`) or landscape (`4:3` or `16:9`). Cursor does not support `2:3`; use `3:4` as the portrait default.

**Gallery set (2–6 prints):** this is ONE coordinated series, not independent images. The set must share a visual anchor — same palette family, same motif language, same ground color and texture, same margin treatment (all artwork-only or all posters). Generate them to hang together: consistent style across the set, with each print varying only the motif/composition. When consistency matters, establish the look with one piece first (or pass a shared reference), then produce the rest against it so they don't each "paint themselves differently."

Build the prompt from the language chosen: name the style, the motif, the treatment, the specific palette, cream ground, hand-made paper/brush texture, and either full-bleed artwork-only or the exhibition-poster layout.

**Reference images:** pass local files via `reference_image_paths` to anchor style/color/texture — treat them as style inspiration, never copying a specific composition or famous work. Borrow the language; invent the forms. Prefer 1–3 anchors that match **both** language and output mode (poster refs for posters, full-bleed refs for artwork-only).

### Cursor GenerateImage

Use the Cursor `GenerateImage` tool (not `generate_media`).

1. Choose language A, B, or C (hybrid only if asked).
2. Choose output mode: artwork-only (default) or exhibition poster.
3. Assemble the prompt from [Prompt building blocks](#prompt-building-blocks); keep the user's subject wording verbatim.
4. Pick 1–3 matching local style anchors. Do not pass all of them.
5. Call `GenerateImage` once per print:
   - `description`: the assembled prompt
   - `aspect_ratio`: `3:4` (default wall art), `1:1` (square), `4:3` or `16:9` (landscape)
   - `filename`: `matisse-{motif-slug}.png`
   - `reference_image_paths`: local files from this skill's `references/` directory (and, for later prints in a set, the first generated image)
6. Do not re-embed generated images as Markdown; the client displays them automatically. Include one `<img>` tag if the tool result provides an artifact path.
7. For a set: generate print 1 first, then pass that file as a shared reference for prints 2–N so palette, ground, and texture stay locked.

## Prompt building blocks

Assemble prompts from these ingredients (adapt to the request; keep user's subject wording verbatim):

**Cut-outs:** "Matisse-style papiers découpés paper cut-out art, flat solid gouache-paper shapes with subtle paper grain and slightly rough hand-cut edges, [motif], bold [colors] on a warm cream/off-white ground, generous negative space, abstract simplified organic forms, no shading or perspective, full-bleed artwork, no text, no border, modern gallery wall art."

**Painterly:** "Matisse-style Fauvist painting, loose expressive brushwork, bright non-naturalistic color, flattened perspective, [scene: open window / interior / garden / figure], [colors], hand-painted edges, warm light, full-bleed artwork, no text, no border, modern fine-art wall print."

**Contour:** "Matisse-style brush-ink contour drawing, thick calligraphic black ink lines on warm cream paper, visible brush pressure, extreme economy of line, [portrait or figure], generous negative space, no shading, no color fill, full-bleed artwork, no text, no border, modern gallery wall art."

**Exhibition poster (append, and drop the 'no text, no border' clause):** "vertical museum exhibition poster, wide warm cream mat, centered high-contrast black serif type, header MATISSE in bold tracked caps over a smaller italic subtitle, inner flat color panel with the artwork, original footer gallery line, no famous-work copy, no lorem ipsum."

For a set, append: "part of a coordinated gallery set, consistent palette, ground, texture and style with the other prints."

## What to avoid

- Reproducing a specific, recognizable Matisse artwork or poster composition — make original forms.
- Copying *The Dance*, Blue Nude, Nadia portraits, named exhibition posters, or their typography.
- Photorealism, 3D rendering, glossy gradients, drop shadows, fine digital detail.
- Cluttered, over-detailed scenes; muddy or neon colors; pure-white sterile grounds (unless asked).
- Adding poster text, titles, signatures, or white borders on artwork-only decor requests.
- Thin digital outlines instead of brush-ink contours; vector-perfect cut-outs.
- Letting prints in a set diverge into different palettes/styles.
